Four parts, one click

When you press Add edit to queue, your request travels through four parts. Everything stays on your Mac, nothing is uploaded.

  1. 🖱️YouPick an image, paint a mask, write what should change.
  2. 🧭Inpaint StudioThis app. Turns your clicks into a recipe and keeps the queue and history.
  3. ⚙️ComfyUIThe engine. Runs the recipe step by step on the Mac's GPU.
  4. 📦Model filesThe trained "brain": a few big files in the models folder.

The recipe is called a graph: small boxes (nodes) connected by wires. You never have to build it yourself, the app does that for you. The next sections open it up.

Inside the recipe

This is the real graph the app sends to ComfyUI for Qwen-Image 2.1. Scroll through the steps, the boxes light up.

  • Painter
  • Reader
  • Translator
  • Add-on
  • Image in / out

  1. Load the files

    Three loader nodes open the painter, the reader and the translator. A LoRA, if you picked one, is clipped onto the painter.

  2. Bring your image

    Start with an empty canvas

    Your picture is scaled to about one megapixel. That is the size the model works best at.

    No input image: the painter starts from a blank latent that is filled with random noise.

  3. Read the prompt

    The reader looks at your text and your image together, so "make the roof red" knows which roof.

    The reader turns your sentence into numbers. Nothing else goes in.

  4. Paint in a loop

    The sampler is where the work happens. It runs the painter again and again, usually 20 times. More on that just below.

  5. Back to pixels

    The translator unpacks the latent into a real image, and it is saved to your results.

Want to see it in ComfyUI itself? In Runs, Download for ComfyUI saves a result with this graph inside. Drop the file onto ComfyUI and the exact graph opens.

The loop

The sampler does not paint in one go. It starts with pure noise and cleans it up a little in every step.

  1. Look at the noisy picture
  2. Guess which part is noise
  3. Remove a bit of it
  4. Next step

Step 0: only noise. The prompt decides what the noise will turn into.

0/ 20 steps
20steps for most models
6–8steps for Turbo models, about 5× faster
LiveRuns shows a preview after every step

The noise at step 0 comes from the seed, a single number. Same seed and same settings give the same picture. A new seed gives a new variation.

Why size matters

The painter does not see pixels. It sees a grid where every cell stands for a 16 × 16 pixel block, called a token.

Working sizeTokens
768 × 5121 536fast
1024 × 10244 096default
1280 × 12806 400gray noise

On a Mac the edit breaks into gray noise above about 4096 tokens. Auto-fix size (on by default) keeps you below that. For small changes in a big photo, Edit around the mask only keeps the full resolution anyway.

Smaller files, almost the same picture

A model is billions of numbers. A quantisation stores each number with fewer bits, like saving a photo as JPEG. The name tells you how many bits.

NameBits per numberQualityUse it when
Q4_K_M~4.5very close to the originalDefault. Fits 16 GB Macs and loads fast.
Q5 / Q65–6a little finer detailYou have RAM to spare.
Q8_08practically identical32 GB or more and you want the last bit.
BF1616the originalRarely worth it, twice the size of Q8.
Q2 / Q32–3visibly worseOnly if nothing else fits.

The K means the smart variant that keeps the most important numbers more precise. M is the medium size of that.

  1. Pick a task. Edit changes a picture, Generate makes one from text, Upscale only enlarges.
  2. Pick a model. Only installed models show up.
  3. Choose an image or a whole folder (every file gets the same edit).
  4. Choose what may change. See the four areas below.
  5. Mark the spot. Type "roof" under Find mask, or paint it with the brush (B), wand (W) or bucket (G).
  6. Say what should happen. Plain instructions work best: "remove the person", "make it sunset".
  7. Add edit to queue (⌘↵). You can prepare the next one while it runs.
